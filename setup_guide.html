TOOL 1

LIHTC Feasibility Underwriting Tool

A browser-based 9% and 4% LIHTC deal underwriting tool. Runs entirely in your browser with no server, no login, and no API keys. Enter your address, import HUD income limits, run Claude Chat for comp research, and get a complete feasibility model with sources & uses, debt sizing, tax credit equity, eligible basis, sensitivity analysis, and gap calculation.

Access: lihtc_feasibility.html

1
Workflow Overview
Four steps from address to completed feasibility model
1
Enter Address & Click ⚡ Analyze

Type your project address — including state abbreviation and ZIP — and click ⚡ Analyze. The tool instantly applies RSMeans regional construction cost factors and operating expense benchmarks for your region, then opens the Claude Chat panel automatically.

2
Import HUD MTSP Income Limits (Setup Tab)

Go to the Setup tab and use the HUD MTSP import panel. Visit huduser.gov → MTSP Income Limits ↗, select your state and county, paste the results page text into the import box, and click ✓ Parse & Import.

Once imported, the LIHTC Gross Max column in the Units tab populates for all unit types at all AMI levels. Toggling AMI% on any row instantly updates gross max and net max — no re-import needed. If paste fails, enter the 4-Person AMI directly in the manual field.

3
Run Claude Chat for Market Research

Click Claude Chat in the top-right of the tool to open the research panel. Copy the pre-filled prompt and paste it into a new claude.ai conversation. Claude searches the web for actual LIHTC comp rents, PHA utility allowances, construction costs, operating expense benchmarks, and soft financing sources for your market. Paste the JSON response back and click ✓ Apply to Model. All fields populate automatically and are highlighted in blue. Soft source suggestions appear in the Financing tab as a reference panel — amounts are not auto-entered and must be confirmed manually.

4
Review, Adjust & Save

Every field is editable and the model recalculates in real time as you type. Check the five summary cards (DSCR, Loan to Cost, Gap, Annual Credit, OpEx/Unit) at the top of the results panel. Click 💾 Save/Load in the header to name and save your analysis, load a prior version, or export/import JSON for sharing.

2
Setup Tab
Income limits, deal type, building assumptions, credit parameters
A
HUD MTSP Income Limits Import

Paste the HUD MTSP results page text and click ✓ Parse & Import. The tool extracts the 50% (Very Low Income) limit row, derives 60% at 1.20×, and computes LIHTC gross max rents for all unit types across all AMI levels (20–80%) using HUD's standard household size adjustment factors.

Tip: If the full page paste fails, try selecting just the income limits table on the HUD page. If it still fails, enter the 4-Person AMI directly in the manual field — the tool computes all limits from that single value.
B
Deal Type: 9% Competitive vs 4% Tax-Exempt Bond

9% deals: The 9% credit rate is used. Eligible basis × boost × 9% × 10 years × equity price = tax credit equity. First mortgage auto-sized from DSCR and LTV targets.

4% deals: The credit rate is locked at 4.00% under TCJA 2017 §42(b)(3) — this is a fixed statutory rate, not the monthly AFR. Tax-exempt bonds are sized to satisfy the 25% aggregate basis test (CAA 2021): bonds target 30% of aggregate basis (land + hard costs + soft costs) and must equal at least 25%. A taxable first mortgage covers any remaining hard debt need. The 25% bond test result is shown in the Sources & Uses panel.

C
QCT / DDA Basis Boost

Toggle the QCT/DDA switch to apply the 130% basis boost. Adjusted eligible basis is multiplied by 1.30 before calculating annual credit — a significant increase in credit equity. Use the QCT/DDA Scanner to verify your site's designation first.

D
Building & Site Assumptions

Enter GBA (gross building area SF), number of stories, and site acreage. The height cost premium is applied automatically: ≤3 stories = 1.00×, 4–6 stories = 1.14×, 7–12 stories = 1.32×, 13–20 stories = 1.50×. The suggested unit count is auto-derived from acreage × density (based on stories). Market-rate unit count (excluded from eligible basis) is entered here.

3

Each row represents one unit type at one AMI level. All computed columns update instantly when AMI% or income limits change.

4
Expenses, Costs & Financing Tabs
Operating pro forma, development budget, debt and equity structure
A
Expenses Tab

All line items are annual per-unit amounts except management (% of EGI), payroll/benefits (total $ fields), and vacancy (% of GPR). Georgia DCA's QAP minimum is $5,500/unit/year for City of Atlanta projects and $4,500/unit/year for other MSAs — the tool displays a warning if your total falls below the applicable minimum.

Replacement reserve defaults to $250/unit/year (DCA minimum). Claude Chat populates all operating benchmarks from market comp data, highlighted in blue.

B
Costs Tab

Hard costs: Enter construction cost as $/unit (primary input) — $/SF is derived. The height premium is applied automatically. Site work, hard cost contingency %, and tap/impact fees are separate line items. FF&E and soft cost contingency default to $800/unit and $1,500/unit respectively if left blank.

Soft costs: Arch & engineering, environmental, title, legal, market study, appraisal, accounting, LIHTC fees, lender inspection, builders risk, real estate taxes during construction, partnership organization, and other soft costs.

Construction financing: Construction loan rate, construction period (months), and permanent loan origination fee % are all entered in the Costs tab. These drive construction interest and origination cost calculations in the Development Budget.

Reserves & developer fee: Operating reserve auto-calculates to 6 months GPR + 6 months OpEx (override available). Lease-up reserve defaults to $500/unit. Developer fee is % of eligible basis with a separate deferred % field.

C
Financing Tab

First mortgage sizing: The model always sizes the first mortgage to produce exactly the DSCR target (default 1.15×). The displayed "First Mtg DS" is therefore constant at NOI ÷ DSCR — it does not change when you adjust the interest rate or amortization. What does change with rate/amortization is the mortgage amount: lower rates and longer amortization allow a larger loan for the same debt service capacity. The Financing tab displays both the mortgage amount and total debt service with live updates.

4% blended debt sizing: For 4% deals, the model solves algebraically for the first mortgage using the blended bond rate + taxable rate, ensuring the bond exactly hits the 30% aggregate basis target while the total first mortgage hits exactly 1.15× DSCR.

Additional hard debt tranches: Mezzanine, second mortgages, USDA RD, or bridge loans can be added with rate, amortization, and P&I or interest-only payment type. All hard debt service counts against DSCR and reduces first mortgage capacity.

Soft sources: Each soft source has a payment type — Deferred/Accruing (no DSCR impact), Simple Interest Only (counts vs DSCR), P&I Amortizing (counts vs DSCR), or Cash Flow/Residual Receipts (no DSCR impact). P&I and IO soft sources are grouped with hard debt in the Sources & Uses panel since they affect first mortgage sizing. All soft source amounts are included in Total Sources.

Soft Source Suggestions: When Claude Chat data is applied, a purple panel appears at the top of the Financing tab showing market-specific soft source recommendations (program name, type, suggested amount, notes). These are display only — enter amounts in the table below after reviewing.

DSCR — Net Operating Income ÷ Total Annual Debt Service. Target is 1.15×. Includes first mortgage + all additional hard debt + any P&I or IO soft sources. The "Total DS" label in the Financing tab and the Income & Expenses panel show this same all-in figure.

Loan to Cost — First mortgage ÷ Total Development Cost. Informational only — the model sizes to DSCR first. Max LTV in the Financing tab is a cap, not a target.

Gap / Surplus — TDC minus total sources (hard debt + equity + all soft source principal + deferred fee). A gap shows as a negative number (−$X, red) — money still needed to close the deal. A surplus shows as a positive number (+$X, green). Deal must reach $0 to be financeable.

Annual Credit — Eligible basis × basis boost × credit rate × 10 years × equity price. For 4% deals the credit rate is locked at 4.00%. Override available if you have an HFA allocation letter.

OpEx / Unit / Year — Total operating expenses ÷ total units. Benchmark: $5,500–$8,000/unit for Georgia LIHTC projects depending on market and building type.

Sources & Uses structure: Hard debt (first mortgage + addl tranches + any P&I/IO soft loans) is grouped at the top with a Total Debt Service row. LIHTC equity follows. Deferred grants and cash flow soft sources appear below equity. Deferred developer fee is last. All amounts in both sections add to Total Sources.

Save, Load & Export
Persisting and sharing your work
A
Auto-Save

The tool automatically saves your work to browser localStorage every 2 seconds. Your model is restored automatically when you reopen the page in the same browser — no action required.

B
Named Saves

Click 💾 Save/Load in the page header, enter a project name, and click Save. Up to 20 named saves are stored in your browser. Load any prior save to restore all fields, unit mix, soft sources, and financing assumptions.

Use Export JSON to download a portable file of your complete model state. Share it via email or Teams and the recipient can use Import JSON to load the exact same model in their browser. This is the recommended method for sharing analyses across team members.

Feasibility Tool — FAQ
Why doesn't debt service change when I adjust the interest rate or amortization?

The model always sizes the first mortgage so that debt service equals exactly NOI ÷ DSCR target. Since the DSCR target and NOI are fixed, the debt service is mathematically constant regardless of rate or amortization. What changes is the mortgage amount — a lower rate or longer term lets you borrow more for the same annual payment. This is correct LIHTC underwriting behavior: you're always maximizing debt within the DSCR constraint.

How is the 4% tax-exempt bond sized?

The model solves algebraically for the first mortgage using a blended rate approach. The tax-exempt bond targets 30% of aggregate basis (land + hard costs + soft costs) to satisfy the 25% bond test under CAA 2021. If 30% of aggregate basis exceeds what 1.15× DSCR can support, the bond is reduced accordingly. A taxable first mortgage fills the gap between the bond and the total hard debt need. The model always sizes the total first mortgage (bond + taxable) to exactly hit the 1.15× DSCR target using the blended mortgage constant.

What is the 4% credit rate?

The 4% credit rate is fixed at 4.00% by statute under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 (TCJA §42(b)(3)). This applies to all projects financed with tax-exempt bonds under §142. It is not the monthly IRS Applicable Federal Rate (AFR) — 4% deals use 4.00% regardless of prevailing interest rates.

Why does the proposed rent always equal the Net Max?

The model defaults proposed rents to the LIHTC statutory ceiling (Net Max = Gross Max − UA) regardless of comp rents. This maximizes modeled income and shows the deal's full upside. The Max Achievable column shows what comparable properties actually charge — if that's below Net Max, the DISC% column and the affordability discount percentage tell you the underwriting risk. You can manually override any proposed rent if you want to model conservative comp-based rents.

How do P&I soft sources affect the first mortgage?

Soft sources with P&I or Interest Only payment types count against DSCR just like hard debt. Their annual debt service is subtracted from NOI ÷ DSCR before sizing the first mortgage — so adding a $10M P&I soft loan reduces the first mortgage by approximately the same debt service capacity. Total DS (first mortgage + soft P&I DS) stays constant at NOI ÷ DSCR. The Sources & Uses panel groups P&I/IO soft sources with hard debt and shows the combined Total Debt Service row.

What's the difference between Eligible Basis and Aggregate Basis?

Eligible Basis — hard construction costs + qualified soft costs (A&E, non-lender soft costs) + developer fee applied to those costs. Excludes land, permanent financing costs, non-qualifying costs, and lender-required costs. Multiplied by the basis boost (130% if QCT/DDA) to get Adjusted Eligible Basis, then multiplied by the credit rate × 10 years × equity price for LIHTC equity.

Aggregate Basis — all hard costs + all soft costs + land. Used only for the 4% bond test: tax-exempt bonds must equal ≥25% of aggregate basis (the tool targets 30%).

Total Development Cost (TDC) — everything: land, hard costs, all soft costs, financing costs, developer fee, operating reserve, lease-up reserve. Used for the gap calculation (TDC − all sources).

What does Claude Chat actually research?

Claude searches the web for: (1) actual LIHTC comp rents at comparable Section 42 properties in your market; (2) local PHA utility allowance schedules; (3) LIHTC operating expense benchmarks from state HFA publications; (4) construction cost data from HFA award applications and RSMeans regional factors; (5) recommended unit mix and total unit count for the submarket; (6) soft financing sources with program-specific suggested amounts based on recent awardees. All fields populate automatically with blue highlights. The soft source panel shows researched programs with suggested amounts — these are not auto-entered and require your review before adding to the Financing tab.

Why does the LIHTC Gross Max show dashes before I import HUD data?

The tool requires actual HUD MTSP income limits for your specific market — state-level averages are too inaccurate for high-AMI metros. Import via the Setup tab or enter the 4-Person AMI manually to populate all Gross Max columns.

How do I model a cash flow residual receipts soft loan?

In the Financing tab, add your soft source and set the payment type to Cash Flow / Residual Receipts. The model treats this as $0 annual debt service for DSCR purposes — standard HFA underwriting treatment. The full principal counts toward Total Sources in the S&U.

Can I share my analysis with a colleague?

Yes. Click 💾 Save/Load in the page header and choose Export JSON to download a portable file. Email it to your colleague; they click Import JSON to load the exact same model in their browser. Named saves are browser-local only — JSON export is the correct method for sharing.

TOOL 2 — QCT & DDA SCANNER
TOOL 2

QCT & DDA Scanner

Batch-check whether LIHTC sites qualify as Qualified Census Tracts (QCT) or Difficult Development Areas (DDA) under HUD's FY2026 designations. Accepts a CSV of addresses or lat/lon coordinates, geocodes via the US Census Bureau API, and queries HUD's live ArcGIS designation services. Results export to CSV. Crexi export format is directly supported.

Access: lihtc_scanner.html

1
How the Scanner Works
Data sources and lookup methodology
1
Geocoding (Address Mode)

Each address is geocoded via the US Census Bureau Geocoder API — free, US-only, returns latitude/longitude and census tract GEOID. The GEOID is used for QCT tract matching; lat/lon is used for the DDA spatial query.

2
QCT Lookup

In address mode, the scanner pre-loads all FY2026 QCT census tract GEOIDs from HUD's ArcGIS service in a single batch call (~2,400 tracts nationally), then checks each geocoded site against the set. In coordinate mode (lat/lon in CSV), it queries the HUD ArcGIS spatial service directly per point.

3
DDA Lookup

A live spatial query to HUD's FY2026 DDA ArcGIS service checks whether each site's lat/lon falls within a designated DDA polygon. This uses HUD's official published boundaries.

4
Results & Export

Results show QCT status, DDA status, census tract GEOID, and geocoded address for each site. Click ↓ Export CSV to download — your original columns plus QCT, DDA (Live), GEOID, and Geocoded Address appended. Filter by designation in Excel to identify your QCT/DDA opportunities.

2
Step-by-Step Usage
From CSV upload to exported results
1
Prepare Your CSV

The scanner accepts any CSV with either: (A) address columns — street address, city, state, ZIP; or (B) coordinate columns — latitude and longitude. A name/label column is recommended.

Crexi export CSVs are directly supported — the scanner skips Crexi's junk header rows and auto-detects the format.

2
Upload & Map Columns

Drag and drop your CSV onto the drop zone or click to browse. The column mapping panel auto-detects likely matches by column name. Confirm the assignments and click ► Run Scan.

3
Monitor Progress

In address mode, the scanner first loads all QCT tracts, then processes each address sequentially with a progress bar and ETA. Failed geocodes appear as "Unknown" in a warnings panel — common causes include non-standard address formats or addresses outside the Census geocoder's coverage.

4
Export Results

Click ↓ Export CSV when the scan completes. The file includes all original columns plus: QCT (Yes/No/Unknown), DDA (Live) (Yes/No/Unknown), GEOID (11-digit census tract ID), and Geocoded Address. Click New Scan to start over.

Scanner — FAQ
What year's QCT and DDA designations does the scanner use?

The scanner uses HUD FY2026 designations loaded directly from HUD's live ArcGIS services — the current designations applicable to projects placed in service in 2026. Because the tool queries the live service rather than cached data, it reflects any updates HUD makes during the year.

Some sites are showing "Unknown" — what does that mean?

Unknown for QCT — the Census geocoder could not resolve the address to a census tract GEOID. Common causes: non-standard address format, very new construction not yet in the Census database, or a rural address with limited geocoder coverage.

Unknown for DDA — the spatial query to HUD's ArcGIS service failed, usually a temporary network issue. Run the scan again or check your connection.

How fast does the scanner process addresses?

Approximately 2–4 seconds per address, processed sequentially to respect rate limits. A list of 50 addresses typically completes in 2–3 minutes. For large lists (500+ addresses), break the CSV into smaller batches.

The scanner is stuck loading QCT tracts. What should I do?

QCT loading requires access to HUD's ArcGIS services at services.arcgis.com. Try: (1) Refresh the page and run again. (2) Check that your network or firewall isn't blocking the ArcGIS domain. (3) Switch to coordinate mode — if your CSV has lat/lon columns, the scanner queries ArcGIS per-point rather than pre-loading all tracts, which may work better on restricted networks.

Does the scanner work with Crexi exports?

Yes. Crexi's CSV export includes 1–2 junk rows before the actual column headers. The scanner detects and skips these automatically, then reads address, city, state, and name columns. Drop the Crexi export directly onto the drop zone.

Is the scanner's QCT/DDA data reliable enough for HFA applications?

The scanner pulls from HUD's official published ArcGIS services — the same underlying data HUD uses. That said, always verify through your state HFA's QAP and the official HUD income limits page before submitting an application. For sites near tract boundaries, a small geocoding offset can change the result — use the GEOID from the export to manually verify the tract at huduser.gov.

Can I scan a single address without a CSV?

The scanner is CSV-based. For a single address, create a one-row CSV (Name, Address, City, State) and upload it. Alternatively, use the LIHTC Feasibility Tool — the QCT/DDA toggle on the Setup tab lets you model the basis boost; verify the designation using the GEOID from a manual Census geocoder lookup.
