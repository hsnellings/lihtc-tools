LIHTC Feasibility Underwriting Tool

A browser-based 9% and 4% LIHTC deal underwriting tool. Runs entirely in your browser with no server, no login, and no API keys. Enter your address, import HUD income limits, run Claude Chat for comp research, and get a complete feasibility model with sources & uses, debt sizing, tax credit equity, eligible basis, sensitivity analysis, and gap calculation.

Access: lihtc_feasibility.html

1
Workflow Overview
Four steps from address to completed feasibility model
1
Enter Address & Click ⚡ Analyze

Type your project address — including state abbreviation and ZIP — and click ⚡ Analyze. This is instant with no network calls. The tool applies RSMeans regional construction cost factors and default operating expense benchmarks for your region, then opens the Claude Chat panel automatically.

2
Import HUD MTSP Income Limits (Setup Tab)

Go to the Setup tab and use the HUD MTSP import panel to load FY2026 income limits for your market. Visit huduser.gov → MTSP Income Limits ↗, select your state and county, paste the results page text into the import box, and click ✓ Parse & Import.

Once imported, the LIHTC Gross Max column in the Units tab populates for all unit types. Toggling AMI% on any unit row (20%–80%) instantly updates the gross max and net max — no re-import needed.

3
Run Claude Chat for Comp Research

The Claude Chat panel opens automatically after Analyze. Copy the pre-filled research prompt and paste it into a new claude.ai conversation. Claude searches the web for actual LIHTC comp rents, local PHA utility allowances, construction costs, operating expense benchmarks, and soft financing sources for your market. Paste Claude's JSON response back and click ✓ Apply to Model. All fields populate automatically and are highlighted in blue.

4
Review, Adjust & Stress Test

Review the populated model across all five tabs. Every field is editable — adjust unit mix, rents, costs, and financing assumptions as needed. The model recalculates in real time. Check the four summary cards (DSCR, LTV, Gap, Annual Credit) at the top of the results panel, then review the Sensitivity Analysis section for rent and construction cost stress tests.

2
Setup Tab
Income limits, deal type, credit assumptions, financing targets
A
HUD MTSP Income Limits Import

Navigate to the HUD MTSP results page for your county, paste the page text into the import panel, and click ✓ Parse & Import. The tool extracts the 50% (Very Low) income limit row, derives 60% limits at 1.20x, and computes LIHTC gross max rents for all unit types across all AMI levels (20–80%).

If paste doesn't parse, enter the 4-Person Area Median Income in the manual field — the tool computes income limits using HUD's standard household size adjustment factors (.70, .80, .90, 1.00, 1.08, etc.).

Tip: If the paste fails, try selecting just the income limits table on the HUD page rather than the entire page, then paste again.
B
Deal Type: 9% vs 4%

9% deals use the 9% credit rate. Eligible basis × boost × 9% × 10 years × equity price = tax credit equity. First mortgage auto-sized from DSCR and LTV constraints.

4% deals use the monthly IRS AFR-based credit rate (enter current rate in the field that appears). Tax-exempt bonds auto-size to the lesser of 30% of eligible basis or the first mortgage need — satisfying the 25% bond test under CAA 2023. A taxable first mortgage covers any remaining first mortgage need.

C
QCT / DDA Basis Boost

Toggle the QCT/DDA switch to apply the 130% basis boost. Eligible basis is multiplied by 1.30 before calculating annual credit — a significant increase in credit equity. Use the QCT/DDA Scanner to verify your site's designation first.

D
Annual Credit Override

If you have a firm LIHTC allocation from an HFA award letter, enter it in the Annual Credit Override field. This bypasses the basis-calculation engine and uses your specified credit amount directly for equity and S&U purposes. Leave blank or zero to auto-calculate from eligible basis.

3

4
Expenses, Costs & Financing Tabs
Operating pro forma, development budget, debt structure
A
Expenses Tab

All expense line items are annual amounts per unit except management (% of EGI) and vacancy (% of GPR). Default fixed costs total $6,000/unit/year across maintenance, utilities, insurance, administration, services, other operating, and replacement reserve. Claude Chat populates these from market comp data highlighted in blue.

B
Costs Tab

Key inputs: GBA (gross building area SF), construction cost/SF (hard cost before contingency), hard cost contingency %, site work, FF&E, arch/engineering %, and all soft cost line items. Land/acquisition, developer fee %, deferred fee %, and operating reserve are also entered here. Claude Chat populates construction cost, land cost per unit, site work, and contingency from market data.

C
Financing Tab

First mortgage is auto-sized to the lesser of the DSCR-constrained maximum (NOI ÷ DSCR target ÷ annual constant) and the LTV-constrained maximum (TDC × max LTV%). DSCR target defaults to 1.15x, max LTV to 85%.

Additional hard debt tranches (bridge, second mortgages) can be added with rate, amortization, and P&I or interest-only payment type. These count against DSCR.

Soft sources (HOME, CDBG, AHP, state gap, local) each have a payment type: Deferred/Accruing (no DSCR impact), Simple Interest Only (counts against DSCR), P&I Amortizing (counts against DSCR), or Cash Flow/Residual Receipts (no DSCR impact).

Feasibility Tool — FAQ
Why does the LIHTC Gross Max show dashes before I import HUD data?

The tool requires actual HUD MTSP income limits for your specific market — state-level averages were removed because they are too inaccurate for high-AMI metros like DC, NYC, or the Bay Area. Import HUD limits via the Setup tab or enter the 4-Person AMI manually to populate the Gross Max column.

The HUD paste isn't parsing. What should I do?

The parser looks for lines containing "50 Percent" or "Very Low" with 4+ numbers in the income limit range. Try these steps in order:

1. Make sure you are on the HUD MTSP results page (after selecting your county) — not the landing page. 2. Try selecting just the income limits table on the HUD page rather than the entire page. 3. If it still fails, enter the 4-Person Area Median Income directly in the manual AMI field below — the tool will compute income limits from that single value.

What exactly does Claude Chat research?

Claude searches the web for: (1) actual LIHTC comp rents at comparable Section 42 properties in your market; (2) the local PHA utility allowance schedule; (3) LIHTC operating expense benchmarks from state HFA publications; (4) construction cost data from HFA award applications and RSMeans regional factors; (5) total development cost per unit at comparable deals; and (6) available soft financing sources (HOME, AHP/FHLB, state gap programs). All data populates automatically with blue highlights. Always verify figures against your own market knowledge before using in an application.

How is the first mortgage sized?

The first mortgage is the lesser of two constraints: (1) DSCR-constrained — (NOI ÷ DSCR target) ÷ annual mortgage constant; and (2) LTV-constrained — TDC × Max LTV%. Default DSCR target is 1.15x and Max LTV is 85%. Both are adjustable in the Financing tab.

How does the 4% / tax-exempt bond calculation work?

For 4% deals, tax-exempt bonds auto-size to the lesser of 30% of adjusted eligible basis (which satisfies the 25% bond test under CAA 2023) or the total first mortgage need. The 4% credit rate is entered manually — check the current IRS Applicable Federal Rate monthly at irs.gov. A taxable first mortgage covers any first mortgage need above the bond amount.

What's the difference between Eligible Basis and Total Development Cost?

Eligible Basis includes hard construction costs, architectural/engineering fees, and qualified soft costs. It excludes land, permanent financing costs, and developer fee (unless deferred). Total Development Cost includes everything: land, hard costs, all soft costs, financing costs, developer fee, operating reserve, and relocation. The gap calculation uses TDC vs. all sources.

How do I model soft debt with residual receipts repayment?

In the Financing tab, add your soft source and set the payment type to Cash Flow / Residual Receipts. The tool treats this as $0 annual debt service for DSCR purposes — standard HFA underwriting treatment. The source still counts toward total sources in the S&U.

Can I save my work?

The tool runs entirely in-browser with no server. Use your browser's File → Save Page As to save the HTML file locally with your inputs preserved. Reopen the saved file in any browser to continue working. The file is fully self-contained and works offline.

TOOL 2 — QCT & DDA SCANNER
TOOL 2NO API KEY REQUIRED

QCT & DDA Scanner

Batch-check whether LIHTC sites qualify as Qualified Census Tracts (QCT) or Difficult Development Areas (DDA) under HUD's FY2026 designations. Accepts a CSV of addresses or lat/lon coordinates, geocodes each site via the US Census Bureau API, and queries HUD's live ArcGIS designation services. Results export to CSV. Crexi export format is directly supported.

Access: lihtc_scanner.html

1
How the Scanner Works
Data sources and lookup methodology
1
Geocoding (Address Mode)

The scanner geocodes each address using the US Census Bureau Geocoder API — free, US-only, returns latitude/longitude and census tract GEOID. The GEOID is used for QCT tract matching; lat/lon is used for DDA spatial lookup.

2
QCT Lookup

In address/geocoding mode, the scanner pre-loads all FY2026 QCT census tract GEOIDs from HUD's ArcGIS service (~2,400 tracts nationally) in a single batch call. It then checks each geocoded GEOID against the set — no network call per address. In coordinate mode (lat/lon in CSV), it queries the HUD ArcGIS spatial service directly per point.

3
DDA Lookup

DDA designations use a live spatial query to HUD's FY2026 DDA ArcGIS service for each site's coordinates. The query checks whether the lat/lon point falls within a designated DDA polygon. This uses HUD's official published boundaries.

4
Results & Export

Results display in a table with QCT status, DDA status, census tract GEOID, and geocoded address. Click ↓ Export CSV to download — original columns plus QCT, DDA (Live), GEOID, and Geocoded Address appended. Filter by designation in Excel to identify your QCT/DDA opportunities.

2
Step-by-Step Usage
From CSV upload to exported results
1
Prepare Your CSV

The scanner accepts any CSV with either: (A) address columns — street address, city, state, and optionally ZIP; or (B) coordinate columns — latitude and longitude. A name/label column is recommended for identifying results in the output.

Crexi export CSVs are directly supported — drop the file and the scanner skips Crexi's junk header rows and auto-detects the format.

2
Upload & Map Columns

Drag and drop your CSV onto the drop zone, or click to browse. The column mapping panel auto-detects likely matches by column name. Confirm the assignments and click ► Run Scan.

3
Monitor Progress

In address mode, the scanner first loads all QCT tracts (shown in the progress panel), then processes each address sequentially with a progress bar and ETA. Failed geocodes appear as "Unknown" in a warnings panel — common causes include non-standard address formats or addresses outside the Census geocoder's coverage.

4
Export Results

When the scan completes, click ↓ Export CSV. The file includes all original columns plus: QCT (Yes/No/Unknown), DDA (Live) (Yes/No/Unknown), GEOID (11-digit census tract ID), and Geocoded Address. Click New Scan to start over with a different file.

?
Scanner — FAQ
What year's QCT and DDA designations does the scanner use?

The scanner uses HUD FY2026 designations loaded directly from HUD's live ArcGIS services — the current designations applicable to projects placed in service in 2026. Because the tool queries the live service rather than cached data, it will reflect any updates HUD makes throughout the year.

Some sites are showing "Unknown" — what does that mean?

Unknown for QCT means the Census geocoder could not resolve the address to a census tract GEOID. Common causes: non-standard address format, very new construction not yet in the Census database, or a rural address with limited geocoder coverage.

Unknown for DDA means the spatial query to HUD's ArcGIS service failed — usually a temporary network issue. Run the scan again or check your connection.

How fast does the scanner process addresses?

The scanner processes addresses sequentially to avoid rate limits on the Census geocoder and HUD ArcGIS services — approximately 2–4 seconds per address. A list of 50 addresses typically completes in 2–3 minutes. For large lists (500+ addresses), break the CSV into smaller batches.

The scanner is stuck loading QCT tracts. What should I do?

QCT loading requires access to HUD's ArcGIS services at services.arcgis.com. Try: (1) Refresh the page and run again. (2) Check that your network or firewall isn't blocking the ArcGIS domain. (3) Switch to coordinate mode — if your CSV has lat/lon columns, the scanner queries ArcGIS per-point rather than pre-loading all tracts, which may work better on restricted networks.

Does the scanner work with Crexi exports?

Yes. Crexi's CSV export includes 1–2 junk rows at the top before the actual column headers. The scanner automatically detects and skips these, then reads address, city, state, and name columns. Drop your Crexi export directly onto the drop zone.

Is the scanner's QCT/DDA data reliable enough for HFA applications?

The scanner pulls data directly from HUD's official published ArcGIS services — the same underlying data HUD uses. That said, always verify designation status through your state HFA's QAP and the official HUD income limits page before submitting applications. For sites near tract boundaries, a small geocoding offset can change the result — use the GEOID from the export to manually verify the tract at huduser.gov.

Can I scan a single address without a CSV?

The scanner is CSV-based. For a single address, create a one-row CSV (Name, Address, City, State) and upload it. Alternatively, use the LIHTC Feasibility Tool with the QCT/DDA toggle on the Setup tab — you can visually confirm designation status using the GEOID from a manual Census geocoder lookup.
